A field guide to language models
Chapter one · The basics

The Shape of Meaning

When you type a sentence to an AI, what does it actually see? Not letters. Not even words. It sees a map — and the closer two ideas live on that map, the more the AI thinks they belong together.

Fig 01 · Cutting words into pieces

First, words get chopped up.

Computers don't read whole words. They cut text into smaller chunks called tokens. Common words stay whole. Long or rare ones get split into pieces.

Try typing something below — even nonsense words — and watch how it splits.

whole word word piece ending other
Fig 02 · Pieces become numbers

Each piece gets a number.

The model has a giant dictionary — usually 30,000 to 100,000 entries. Every piece has its own ID. That's it. From here on, the AI works with numbers, never letters.

Fig 03 · A map of meaning

Then numbers become positions on a map.

Here's the magic step. Each ID gets turned into a position in a vast space. Words with similar meanings end up close together. Words about different things end up far apart.

Real models use thousands of dimensions, but here's a flat, two-dimensional version so you can see what's happening:

Fig 04 · Dimensions as features

Each direction means something.

A position on the map isn't random. Each direction (each "dimension") tracks something — like how alive a thing is, or how big, or how warm. Pick a word and see how it scores on a few sample dimensions:

In a real model there aren't four directions — there are thousands. And nobody hand-picks what they mean. The model figures them out by reading huge amounts of text.

Fig 05 · Doing math with meaning

Now the strange part.

Once words are positions, you can do arithmetic on them. Add and subtract meanings the way you'd add and subtract numbers. The most famous example:

king − man + woman = queen

Take "king," subtract the meaning of "man," add the meaning of "woman" — you land on "queen." Nobody told the model that. It learned the shape of meaning from billions of sentences, and royalty plus gender just lined up.

Every token sits inside a space with thousands of directions. The "shape of meaning" is wildly more detailed than anything we can draw. But the idea is the same: closeness equals relatedness.

Fig 07 · Putting it together

So when you type a sentence…

…the model chops it into pieces, looks each one up, finds its position in the meaning-space, and then uses all those positions together to figure out what should come next. The whole conversation — every reply you've ever read from an AI — boils down to geometry.

Words you thought were just symbols turn out to have a shape. And meaning, it turns out, is a place.
