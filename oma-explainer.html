How a Computer Reads(told the simple way)

You know those clever talking computers — the ones that write letters and answer questions? Here is what they really do, in five small steps. No big words. Just kitchen things.

~ Step 1 ~

First, the computer slices the words.

Think of an apple. If it's small, you can eat it whole. If it's big, you cut it into pieces.

A computer does the same with words. Short, common words it keeps whole — like cat, or dog. Long or unusual words get sliced into smaller bites.

"Try it yourself, dear — type a long word and watch."

whole little word word piece ending
~ Step 2 ~

Every slice gets a number.

Imagine a very long street. Every house has a number — number 7, number 12, number 144 — so the postman knows where to go.

The computer does the same. Every word-slice gets its own number. After this, the computer doesn't think about letters anymore. Only numbers. That's how it likes to think.

~ Step 3 ~

Then it plants a garden of meaning.

Now here's the lovely part. Imagine a big garden. The computer plants every word in this garden — and words that belong together are planted side by side.

The cats grow next to the dogs. The bread grows next to the cheese. The bicycles grow far away from the strawberries. Have a look:

It learned this all by itself, just by reading and reading and reading. Words that often appear together end up close together. Like little plants that like the same soil.

~ Step 4 ~

It sorts everything by qualities.

You know how you might sort the spice rack? By color. Or by which is sweetest. Or by which is the spiciest.

The computer does the same — but with hundreds of qualities at once. Pick something below, and see where it lands on a few:

~ Step 5 ~

And then — the recipe trick.

When you cook, you sometimes swap an ingredient. You take a recipe for milk coffee, take out the milk, add soy milk — and you have soy coffee. Same drink, just with a swap.

The computer can do this with words. It actually swaps meanings the way you swap ingredients. Watch:

a queen is just a king, but a woman

Take "king." Remove the "man" part. Put in the "woman" part. The answer is "queen." Nobody told the computer this — it figured it out, all on its own, just by reading.

"Like swapping butter for olive oil. The shape of the recipe stays the same."

~ ✿ ~

So that's the whole secret. Slice the words. Number the slices. Plant a garden of meanings. Sort by quality. Swap ingredients.

Five little steps. Done a few thousand times. Across a few billion sentences.

And out comes a computer that seems to understand you.
